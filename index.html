01The premise

You can't shout motivation into a fourteen-year-old. It turns up when they feel understood, and then it tends to stay.

Most of the advice aimed at young people is about effort. Try harder, want it more, push through. Some of them can. A lot of them hear it as proof that the adult in front of them hasn't been paying attention.

Chloe's coaching starts further back. Before any talk of goals or training plans, she wants to know who this person is when nobody is grading them, and what they care about badly enough to get up early for. The performance work is built on top of that answer.

03How it works

Four stages. The first one takes longest, on purpose.

  1. 01 / Notice

    See them

    Early sessions are mostly listening. What do they light up about? When do they go quiet? What do they say about themselves that isn't true?

  2. 02 / Name

    Find the thing

    Together they put words to what actually drives this young person. It often surprises the adults around them.

  3. 03 / Strike

    Light it

    Small goals the young person picks for themselves, set just out of reach. The first win counts for more than its size.

  4. 04 / Sustain

    Keep it lit

    Selection days, exams, injuries, bad weeks. They rehearse the moments that usually put the fire out, so those moments lose some of their power.

05What sits under all of it

Being seen changes how hard you're willing to try.

Chloe watched this happen in classrooms for eighteen years and felt it herself in a Great Britain vest. Young people go further for the adults who have bothered to understand them. Her coaching is built around being that adult, for long enough that they learn to do it for themselves.
